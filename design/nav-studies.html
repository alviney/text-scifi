Seedship · interface study

Where am I in this leg?

The voyage is five harvest seasons. The nav panel's whole job is to answer one question at a glance — how far through the current cluster am I, and what is still ahead of me — with the three hundred years kept firmly in the background where it belongs.

Three treatments of the same moment: Leg 1, The Departure Belt. Day 34 of 90. Five objects, unevenly spaced — two behind, three ahead. Same data, same palette, same device width.

VoyageFacilitiesCrewRules
  • The bar is the season, so 38% means something concrete: 56 days left, three rocks.
  • Marker size is the estimated haul — the big one at d41 reads before any number does.
  • Worked rocks stay on the rail behind you, filled and grey. The season accumulates.
  • Costs: at 26 objects (what the sim actually generates today) the rail crowds badly. This wants 5–8 per leg, which is a real balance decision, not a display one.

VoyageFacilitiesCrewRules
  • Scales to any object count — 26 rocks is a scroll, not a collision.
  • The next one is highlighted in place, so "what do I survey now" answers itself.
  • Costs: size has to be a number rather than a shape, so "a big one is coming" stops being pre-attentive — which was the point of putting rocks on screen at all.

VoyageFacilitiesCrewRules
  • Reads as an instrument rather than a chart — closest to the CLI aesthetic.
  • Buys the most vertical room back for everything else the tab has to carry.
  • Costs: an arc is worse than a line at "how much is left", and the five legs shrink to pips, which is the one thing this screen was asked to make loud.

The recommendation is A. It is the only one where the leg is the picture rather than a caption, and marker size does the work a number can't. The thing it exposes is worth knowing either way: the rail only reads at 5–8 objects per leg, and LEGS[0] currently generates 26. Either the clusters get smaller or the rail needs to summarise, and that is a decision about the harvest, not about the panel.
