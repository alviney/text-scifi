What the bands are doing

  • The object detail matches the hero's height, so the panel reads as two equal plates — where you are, and what is coming — rather than a map with a footnote.
  • The asteroid is drawn, not iconic. A seeded silhouette with real craters and a slow tumble, so the d41 rock is that rock every time you come back to it. The unsurveyed one is unlit — you have a shape and nothing else, which is the survey mechanic stated in a picture.
  • Goals collapse to one line. "Critical tasks 2/5" is the whole summary; the five rows and their detail live one tap in. That buys the room the object needed.
  • Every note has an author. The ticker is the ship talking — it rotates and forgets. The board is the crew talking: a room, a name, a day, and something that person did or noticed. Nothing gets posted there by the ship itself, which is what stops the season reading as a log and starts it reading as a record.
  • One exception, once. At day 0 no crew member has done anything yet, so the only thing on the board is a letter from Mission Control — the people who built you and then watched you leave, thirty-one years ago by their clocks, all of them long past caring whether you answer. Premise, the five legs, the crew already thawing, and the one goal that matters. It is the only long-form text in the game and the only note the crew did not write.

How big is a rock?

Today a unit is an abstract mass unit and a haul is 1,080–3,720 of them in the Departure Belt, 432–1,488 out in the Deep Field, and rich again at Arrival Debris. Nobody can hold that. Dividing by 200 puts every rock in the voyage between 2 and 19, and the leg you are in still reads off the size — a fat belt rock is 18, a Deep Field rock is 5.

Water has nowhere to go. Across a full voyage the fleet lands about 34,800 units of it. 14,400 is spent as drone propellant — and that is netted off inside the harvest before anything reaches the bay, so the player never sees the transaction. The remaining 20,400 has no sink at all. Air is a function of the oxygen generator and the power bus; it does not drink. Grow beds do not drink. Food is rations and crops. So the Cargo Bay caps water at 400 units and the crew leave the rest floating, which is a deliberate rule in logistics.ts — without it, ice fills the bay and starves the fabricator.

That is the actual finding. The most abundant thing in the belt is the one thing the ship does not need, so a quarter of every haul is scenery and the comets — ten objects across the voyage — are rocks you would rather skip. Giving water real sinks (life support drawing it, beds consuming it per crop, propellant as a visible line rather than a hidden deduction) would make a comet a good day and put a second clock next to the ration counter. It is a simulation change, so it is written down here rather than made.

The one thing this does not fix. A part costs 6.67 ore, so the refining chain is a divide-by-thirty: whatever scale the haul reads at, parts are thirty times more numerous. Rescaling moves the big number from the rock to the workshop rather than removing it. The real simplification is to collapse the chain — drop refined metal as a separate store and let ore make parts directly at, say, two parts per unit — which puts hauls at 2–19, parts at 4–40, and takes a material type out of the game. That is a change to the simulation, so it is a decision rather than a mock-up, and I have not made it.
